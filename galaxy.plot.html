How the Clock Works

A record player turns every groove at once. A Sun on an outer track has much farther to travel, so it must move much faster in miles per hour, and it arrives at the same time as the inner one. Same rate, different speed. That is what Leavitt implied, and it is what this plot shows.

For that to happen the Speed has to rise in direct proportion to the Track Number. The Life Track sets the rate for the whole Galaxy, because that is the Track the Torah says supports Life:

Life Track Max Speed = ( aveIteration × |Engines| ) − ( pRate + Life Track )
                     = ( 2012 × 333 ) − ( 2664 + 666 ) = 666,666 mph
Life Track Min Speed = ( minIteration × |Engines| ) + Life Track
                     = (  999 × 333 ) + 666         = 333,333 mph

666,666 ÷ 666 = 1001      333,333 ÷ 666 = 500.5

Max Speed(n) = 1001  × n
Min Speed(n) = 500.5 × n

1001 is 7 × 11 × 13, and 500.5 is 1001 halved.
Multiplying by 1001 is what repeats a three digit number, which is why the whole table now reads the way the Life Track always did:

track  333  :  333,333 mph   3.33333 Hz
track  666  :  666,666 mph   6.66666 Hz   <-- the Life Track
track  999  :  999,999 mph   9.99999 Hz

Why a Frequency, and not a Centrifugal force

A Centrifugal model gives no reason for every Sun to share a rate. Balance a pull inward against a throw outward and what you get depends on where you are standing, so every ring keeps its own time.

A Frequency is a rate. If Gravity is a Frequency, the same as Light, then one rate runs the whole Galaxy and every Sun on it, which is exactly what the straight line above is showing. That is why the measurements here are Frequencies and wavelengths, taken from an electromagnetic system, and the Sun's Plasma is the proof such a system is there to measure.

Is the orbit reasonable

The Life Track works out to a radius of about 27.4 thousand light years, and the Sun is measured to sit 26 to 27 thousand light years from the center of the Galaxy. That is inside 3 percent.
The outermost Track comes to about 54.8 thousand light years, and the Milky Way disk is measured at roughly 50 thousand.

Speed on a Track is a range, not one number. On Track 666 a Sun travels between 333,333 and 666,666 mph, which averages 499,999.5 mph, or 223.5 km/s, against a measured 220 to 250 km/s. At that average the orbit takes 231 million years, against a measured galactic year of 225 to 250 million.

The dots on this plot move at the Max Speed end of the range, so one turn here is 173 million years, the short end. Every Sun varies to the same degree, so the Clock stays the same and only the speed changes.

The Tracks show the movement of the Suns. They are not the Spiral Arms. The Arms show the path of the debris, which is a different path and should not be read as the same.
